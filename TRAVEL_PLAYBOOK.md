# George & Doug Travel Playbook

Read this before planning any trip, check the draft itinerary against it before booking, and add a log entry after each trip.

Last updated: November 2026, after South Korea 2026.

## How to use this

1. **Before planning:** reread the planning rules below, and each pick two or three must-dos.
2. **While drafting:** give every day a day type from the menu. No two days in a row should share a type.
3. **Before booking:** run the pre-trip checklist.
4. **After the trip:** both take the debrief quiz separately, add a row to the trip log, and update the rules if something new came up.

## What makes a trip good for us

Learned from the South Korea 2026 debrief, where we both rated the trip 3 out of 5.

The trip was too repetitive, not too hard. Too many days followed the same pattern: a new neighborhood, an interesting restaurant, and cute shops. Each piece was fine, but repeating it wore us both down.

**What we loved: doing things, adventure, and people**

- Visiting a pottery studio and buying pottery
- Hikes and nature (volcanic cones, lava tubes, the coast)
- Booked hands-on experiences, like a color analysis session
- Unplanned adventure (re-routing around a typhoon)
- Meeting people and nightlife (tteokbokki with a DJ)

**What wore us down**

- Neighborhood walks where the only thing to do was shop
- The same day shape over and over, and a pace that was a bit too packed
- The back half of the trip, when days had the fewest real activities

**What did not bother us (don't over-correct)**

- Re-planning on the fly. We both found it fun.
- Moving between cities and hotels. Both of us said it was fine.
- Language barriers and places with unconfirmed hours. Both rated low stress.

## Planning rules

1. **Every day gets one anchor activity.** That means something we *do*: a class, workshop, hike, tour, treatment, show, or day trip. The neighborhood is just where it happens.
2. **Restaurants and shopping are side dishes.** Plan at most one destination meal per day and keep the rest casual. Shopping fits around the anchor and is never the whole day.
3. **Rotate day types.** No two days in a row of the same type (see the menu below).
4. **Keep the second half strong.** Put at least one top must-do in the final third of the trip.
5. **Plan at least one night out.** Look up music, bars, or events before the trip.
6. **Each person owns days.** Each of us picks two or three must-dos and fully plans one or two days. Doug's days can be fully scheduled.
7. **Leave room for re-planning.** We enjoy adjusting on the go, so a draft can stay loose. Leave one open half-day per city.

## Day-type menu

Label each day in the itinerary with one of these types.

| Day type | What it looks like | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hands-on | A class, workshop, or studio visit | Pottery or craft class, cooking class, seal carving |
| Outdoors | A hike, coast, park, or day trip into nature | Volcanic cone hike, lava tube, coastal walk |
| Experience | A booked session, tour, or show | Color analysis, spa, guided tour, performance |
| Social / night | Built around an evening out | Live music, bar crawl, night market, a DJ set |
| Food | One destination meal plus markets and street food | Fish market breakfast, a tasting menu |
| Culture | A museum, palace, or temple | A palace visit, a museum, a temple stay |
| Slow / free | Wander, rest, shop as you go | Neighborhood stroll, café, hotel time |

A good default for each day: **one anchor + one meal + one flexible slot.**

## Pre-trip checklist

- [ ] Each person listed two or three must-dos, and they're on the calendar
- [ ] Every day has a day type and an anchor activity
- [ ] No two days in a row share a type
- [ ] At most one destination restaurant per day
- [ ] At least one night out is planned
- [ ] The final third of the trip has a top must-do
- [ ] Each city has an open half-day
- [ ] Doug has fully planned at least one day
- [ ] Checked for concerts, marathons, festivals, and weather that could affect traffic or access
